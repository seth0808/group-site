West Virginia,
The Time Traveler

☙

Say I am a time traveler, sent from the porch of my grandmother's cabin far back into the past, before that house was built, before the red creek wound its way through the hollers of West Virginia. Say I am sent back 1 billion years ago, when the Appalachian mountains began to rise from the primordial sea, when ancient continents collided and forced the rocks to fold upon themselves, to rise, over hundreds of millions of years, to form a range thousands of miles long and taller than the Himalayas.

Say you move forward in time, as the plates shifted, and the mountains began to erode back down into the earth, the great range split across three continents, only echoes of similarity in their geography, if you know what you're looking for. Imagine the first Indians, 15,000 years ago, who had made their way across the great flat expanse of the middle of the continent, who reached these mountains and faced the creatures who lived within them: massive sloths and saber-toothed tigers, now-extinct beasts who hid in the narrow valleys and melting glacial plains.

Imagine the frontiersmen who fought the Indians, killed for the right to carve out a little farm in the depths of the mountains, survive long enough to hand it off to his child, who might mine coal, or keep cattle.

✦   ✦   ✦

When my grandfather bought this place in 1974, they used to go to church in Davis, where congregants would speak in tongues and worship with snakes wrapped around their arms. The mountains hold secrets, make men crazy, or at least keep people separated from the slow march of progress which stamps out those ideas. My grandfather's ashes were scattered in the field above the cabin. I tell myself I feel his presence when I am out there; maybe I am infected with the same backwards superstition that leads worshipers to speak in forgotten languages. Maybe it's all in my head..?

I watched my friends catch a fish in the same creek where my grandpa taught me to fish when I was three or four. We played beer pong on the table I used to sit with my family and eat after spending the day at the river. My friends, adults, packed into the same bunk beds my brother and cousins would stay up whispering.

✦   ✦   ✦

In one billion years the cabin will be gone, my bones gone, any memory of this place will not exist. The Appalachian mountains will have receded back into the sea, replaced by new continents and species. But my children will fish that river, my friends will sit around campfires in that valley, and my grandfather's memory will linger in the bones of that house. In 30 years, maybe, my kids will play beer pong with their friends outside that house. By then, the scientists will have invented a time machine, and I will begin my journey back into the past, first to the ancient origins of the mountain range, to the first encounters with Indians, to 15,000 years ago, where I will die having been eaten by a giant sloth. And I will think back on my life, which will be in the future, and remember how I whooped you all in beer die in West Virginia.
