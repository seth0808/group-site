THE FISH WILL BE MADE WHOLE AGAIN

On a hot day in Appalachia, the eight boys played outside the house. They kicked the ball through the grass, they threw the dice high in the air, and one boy ran naked through the field. When they were hungry they cooked what they wanted. When they were sleepy they napped in the shade. They were alone at the house, they were alone in the hills, and they didn't know what they had done.

Later in the afternoon the boys gathered in the front yard by the table. Some sprayed fuel into the fire, some shared stories, and one worked extra in the kitchen to make the meal just right. Once the food was cooked and the meal was prepared, the boys sat at the table. They ate as the sun set.

One boy was eating a fish from the river. The fish's head lay on a plate that had been left on the ground. The fish head spoke loudly:

“The grass is green and the sky is blue, this is a view I had never wished. My head is here, my body by you, cursed are those who have fished.”

The boys backed away. Some fell to the ground in shock. The fish was dead, yet it spoke again. This time more sinisterly:

"You boys have separated my parts at where I bend, I have no digits, yet you have ten. Unfortunately for you, this is not my end, I will be made whole again."

This was too much for one boy. He grabbed the plate and threw it far into the tall grass. The others hurried into the house, leaving their things outside.

It was now dark.

The boys were quiet that night. Some spoke softly. Some were silent so they could listen. They listened for the fish head far out in the grass. Eventually they became too drowsy and fell asleep.

All except one boy. His bed was closest to the front yard. He could hear the fish speaking.

The boy was told to wait until all were in a deep sleep. Then he walked out of the house. He stepped through the tall grass and knelt down into it. The head spoke to him.

The next morning the boys woke with unease. They decided they would leave that moment and packed their bags. They quickly said their goodbyes and parted ways, starting their journey back home.

Hours into his own journey, one boy began remembering the night before. He recalled speaking to the fish in the tall grass. He recalled bringing the fish back to the house. He recalled placing the fish in the bag of another boy so the fish could follow him home.

THE FISH WILL BE MADE WHOLE AGAIN.
