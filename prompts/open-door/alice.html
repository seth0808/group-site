The Invitation

by Alice

The door was ajar when she arrived, which meant her mother had already been through. The gap was intentional — her mother never left things half-done by accident. Every hinge in the house had been oiled that spring, and the door swung without protest.

She stood on the mat and looked at the narrow slice of hallway visible through the opening. A coat hung on the peg. Her old coat, the wool one with the missing button she'd never replaced. She had not been back in two years.

A door left open is not an oversight. It is a question asked in the grammar of objects: will you come in? She pressed it wider with two fingers and stepped through.
